01 — Start here

Know what your wallet is asking you to approve

Before confirming a transaction, understand where it goes, which network it uses and what permission you are granting. Your wallet is the signing tool; the transaction details still need your attention.

THE IDEA

Cold storage reduces online exposure

A hardware wallet is one common approach: it stores private keys in a dedicated device and signs transactions there. You still need to verify what you are approving on the device screen.

THE RESPONSIBILITY

Self-custody means you own the backup risk

If a recovery phrase is lost, exposed or entered on a fake website, a device alone may not protect your funds. Anyone with the phrase can usually restore the wallet.

!A wallet never needs your recovery phrase to “verify,” “unlock” or “validate” it online. Never enter it into a website, chat, support form or screen-sharing session.

01 / LEARN

Start with your threat model

Think about what you need to protect, how often you transact, and what you could safely recover if a device is lost.

02 / VERIFY

Check the maker and the source

Buy from the manufacturer or an explicitly authorized seller. Confirm setup instructions using the maker’s official website.

03 / PRACTICE

Test recovery before relying on it

Follow the manufacturer’s guidance and confirm you understand the recovery process before storing meaningful value.

03 — Before you transact

Before you sign: 5 checks to run

Small pauses can help prevent costly mistakes. Use this as a reminder, not a guarantee of safety.

Never share your seed phrase, private key, PIN or wallet password. Legitimate support staff should not ask you to reveal them.

04 — Scam watch

Common pressure tactics to recognize

Scammers impersonate real wallet brands and support teams. These examples are general warnings and do not imply wrongdoing by the named brands.

FAKE SUPPORT

“We can recover your funds”

A stranger promises to trace or restore lost crypto, then asks for an upfront fee, remote access or your recovery phrase. Recovery-fee demands are a serious red flag.

PHISHING & IMPERSONATION

A familiar logo, an unfamiliar link

Fake support accounts, sponsored search results and copycat apps can imitate MetaMask, Trust Wallet, Coinbase/Base, Bitget, OneKey and others.

MALICIOUS APPROVALS

“Connect to claim your reward”

An unsolicited airdrop or token may lure you to a malicious site or a transaction you do not understand. A token’s name or logo does not prove it is legitimate.

Important: These cards describe scams that impersonate wallet brands. They are not allegations that the named providers are involved in fraud. Verify current instructions through the provider’s official app or website.
TokenPocketFAKE SUPPORT · FAKE AIRDROP

A group “admin” messages you privately

An impersonator may offer to “fix” a wallet issue, ask for an unlock fee, or send an airdrop link or form that requests your recovery phrase. They may also direct you to a phishing site.

Protect yourself Don’t open links from unsolicited DMs. Verify the full receiving address character by character; don’t copy it blindly from transaction history.
Warning signs: unsolicited private messages, pressure to pay an unlock fee, or requests to scan a QR code or send funds to a specified address.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop contact; don’t click, sign, or transfer.
imTokenFAKE APP · FAKE SUPPORT

A look-alike wallet in search results

Copycat apps may reuse a wallet’s name and icon to trick people into importing a wallet. Fake support may contact you by text, phone, or social media and claim you need an upgrade, verification, or recovery.

Protect yourself Reach the app store from the provider’s official website and check the developer name. Start support requests yourself through the official app or site.
Warning signs: mismatched developer details, extra characters in a domain, or anyone asking for your recovery phrase or private key.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop contact; don’t click, sign, or transfer.
TronLinkFAKE TOKEN · FAKE QR CODE

“Arbitrage,” swaps, or airdrops that ask you to send funds

Imitation apps or tokens can use names similar to popular assets. A fake promotion may ask you to send USDT, connect to a DApp, or scan a QR code. Matching token names do not mean matching contract addresses.

Protect yourself Verify app sources, token contract addresses, and networks through official channels. Ignore unsolicited “support” QR codes.
Warning signs: guaranteed arbitrage, a transfer required to unlock a reward, or a token page asking you to connect to an unfamiliar DApp.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop contact; don’t click, sign, or transfer.
OneKeyTAMPERED DEVICE · PRESET PHRASE

A hardware wallet that arrives “already initialized”

A replaced or tampered device, or a card with a pre-written recovery phrase, could expose the wallet to someone else. Fake firmware pages may also imitate update instructions.

Protect yourself Initialize the device yourself using its official instructions. A recovery card should be blank; get firmware only through the official app.
Warning signs: a preset PIN, words already written on the recovery card, or setup instructions telling you to use a phrase supplied by someone else.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop setup and contact the manufacturer through its official site.
Bitget WalletPHISHING LINK · FAKE SUPPORT

“Prize,” airdrop, or “account problem” alerts

Fake sites, texts, QR codes, and support accounts use urgency to push you to log in, connect a wallet, or reveal sensitive information. Fake ads may lead to copycat download pages.

Protect yourself Check the address manually. Contact support from the official app or website. Avoid unknown QR codes and installation files.
Warning signs: prize countdowns, account-freeze threats, unknown QR codes, or login pages on unofficial domains.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop contact; don’t click, sign, or transfer.
MetaMaskFAKE SUPPORT · MALICIOUS APPROVAL

A “support engineer” asks for your recovery phrase

Scammers may pose as support on social platforms or direct you to fake extensions and websites. They may also ask you to sign an unfamiliar message or approve a broad token allowance.

Protect yourself Start support requests from the official support site. Verify extension sources and read the approval target and scope before signing.
Warning signs: requests for recovery words, remote-control software, or a signature that doesn’t match the action you intended.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop contact; don’t click, sign, or transfer.
Base AppFAKE TOKEN · FAKE REWARD

A “Base official token” or limited-time claim

Scammers may misuse Base or Coinbase branding to promote fake tokens, early access, or reward campaigns and then ask you to connect a wallet, sign, or reveal recovery information. An unsolicited token doesn’t require visiting its linked site.

Protect yourself Confirm campaigns through official Base or Coinbase announcements. Never submit a recovery phrase for an airdrop or sign a transaction from an unknown source.
Warning signs: claims of an “official Base token” that require an approval or recovery phrase to claim a reward.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop contact; don’t click, sign, or transfer.
Trust WalletFAKE APP · MALICIOUS DAPP

A fake “wallet verification” page asks for your phrase

Impersonators may claim your wallet needs syncing, verification, or recovery. A phishing DApp may ask you to connect and sign a transaction that moves assets.

Protect yourself Install only from official sources. Reach support from the official site. Check a DApp’s domain and read the transaction details before signing.
Warning signs: a verification page requesting recovery words, or unexpected transfer and approval requests after connecting.
Conclusion: Treat these signs as a scam attempt. Stop contact; don’t click, sign, or transfer.

05 — Quick answers

Questions people often ask

Does a cold wallet physically store my coins?

No. Crypto assets are recorded on their respective blockchains. A wallet stores or protects the keys that let you authorize transactions.

Can a hardware wallet be hacked?

No device is risk-free. Fake apps, unsafe backups, malicious approvals, compromised computers and physical attacks can all create risk. Keep firmware and setup steps tied to the manufacturer’s verified instructions.

What should I do if my recovery phrase was exposed?

Assume the wallet may be compromised. From a trusted device and a verified wallet, seek reputable official guidance to move remaining assets to a newly created wallet with a new recovery phrase. Never send the exposed phrase to anyone for help.
